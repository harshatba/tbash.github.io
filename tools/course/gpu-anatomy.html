What a GPU actually is: memory, all the way down

A GPU is a few hundred tiny processors bolted to a big, slow pool of memory — with two tiny-but-fast tiers wedged between them. This one picture is the physical stage every trick in Part 2 was playing on: the weights live far away in VRAM, and dragging them up is the whole cost.

⏱ ~6 min 🎚 does it fit? computed live math: some

A GPU is a few hundred tiny processors — SMs (Streaming Multiprocessors, ~108 on an A100) — wired to a big, slow pool of memory: VRAM / HBM, 80 GB at 2 TB/s. Between the SMs and that pool sit two tiny-but-fast tiers: on-chip SRAM (~192 KB per SM) and registers.

Everything the model is lives in VRAM: the frozen weights (13.2 GB), the KV cache (Part-2 lesson 3), the activations. The SMs can only compute on what's already been dragged up into SRAM and registers first.

Here's the wall. SRAM is ~10× faster than VRAM but holds only kilobytes, so a decode step must stream all 13.2 GB of weights up from VRAM just to do a little math — VRAM bandwidth is the bottleneck, exactly the "bandwidth-bound" of Part-2 lesson 1. Every move in Part 2, and FlashAttention next page, is a play against this hierarchy.

Show me the actual numbers

The A100, tier by tier. 108 SMs do the multiply-adds. Each SM has its own 192 KB of SRAM (~19 TB/s) and a 256 KB register file. All 80 GB of HBM VRAM sit off to the side at 2.0 TB/s — huge, but ~10× slower to read than SRAM (19 / 2.0 = 9.5×). That gap is the whole story.

The 6.6 ms. A batch-1 decode step reads all 13.2 GB of weights up from VRAM to do ~13 GFLOP of math. At 2.0 TB/s the fetch alone takes 13.2e9 / 2.0e12 ≈ 6.6 ms — the exact number from Part-2 lesson 1. The math is trivial; the fetch is the cost. Only the 56 KiB Flash tile is small enough to sit on-chip while it's worked on — which is precisely the door FlashAttention walks through next page.

This is the roofline (Part-2 lesson 8) rendered in hardware: below the ridge point you're waiting on memory, and the memory you're waiting on is this VRAM, two tiers down from the SMs.

MythA GPU is fast because it has lots of fast memory.
RealityIt has a little fast memory (SRAM, kilobytes on-chip) and a lot of slow memory (VRAM, 80 GB at 2 TB/s). The art of fast inference is keeping the SMs fed across that gap — moving as few bytes as possible up the hierarchy.
Takeaway

A GPU is a memory hierarchy: registers → 192 KB SRAM → 80 GB VRAM, fast-and-tiny to slow-and-huge. The SMs compute, but only on what's dragged up into SRAM first — and VRAM bandwidth is the wall that makes batch-1 decode bandwidth-bound. Only a sliver (a 56 KiB Flash tile) ever fits on-chip; the 13.2 GB of weights never do.

Why is a batch-1 decode step "bandwidth-bound"?
Why: SRAM is tiny and fast, VRAM is huge and slow. A decode step drags every weight up from VRAM (~6.6 ms just to move 13.2 GB), so bandwidth — not the SMs' math — is the wall.
