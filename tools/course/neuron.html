One neuron: multiply, add, squish

A single neuron takes a few numbers, multiplies each by its own weight, adds them up, and bends the total through a curve. That is the whole operation — billions of these are the model.

⏱ ~5 min 🎚 interactive math: some

A neuron takes a few input numbers, multiplies each by its own weight, adds them up, adds a bias, and passes the total through a squish. Every multiply is just input · weight — that is the entire operation, wired up billions of times over.

Why squish at the end? Its real name is the activation function (we use tanh). It bends the straight-line sum into a curve and keeps the answer in a tidy range. Without it, stacking neurons collapses into one straight-line step — ten layers in a row would still be a single straight line, and the network could not model anything curved.

TRY → Drag x₁ all the way up and watch the sum climb past the edges — yet tanh flattens the output near +1. Flip to no activation and the same neuron becomes a plain straight line that shoots off the chart, unbounded.

Show me the actual numbers

At the defaults x = [0.90, 0.30, 0.60] with weights w = [1.3, −0.9, 0.7] and bias 0.1:

0.90 × 1.3 = 1.17,  0.30 × −0.9 = −0.27,  0.60 × 0.7 = 0.42.  Sum: 1.17 + (−0.27) + 0.42 + 0.1 = 1.42.  Squish: tanh(1.42) = 0.89.

Drag x₁ to a large value and the sum keeps growing, but tanh flattens near ±1 — that is the squish saving the number from blowing up. With no activation the neuron is only out = Σxw + b, a straight line; ten of them stacked is still one straight line. The curve is what lets stacked layers build up nonlinear meaning. The whole computation is one line of code: out = Math.tanh(x.reduce((a,xi,i)=>a+xi*w[i],0)+b).

MythA neuron “decides” or “thinks” about its inputs.
RealityA neuron multiplies its inputs by weights, adds them, and squishes the total. The intelligence is in the pattern of billions of these, not in any one.
Takeaway

A neuron is multiply, add, squish — and the squish (a nonlinear activation) is exactly what lets stacked layers model curves instead of collapsing back into one straight line.

What does the activation function (“squish”) buy you?
Why: without a nonlinear squish, any stack of neurons collapses to a single straight-line transform — the curve is what makes depth worth having.
